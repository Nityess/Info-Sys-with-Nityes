Hacking in detail

Hacking uses specialist software and other tools to get in without permission and take administrative control of the network.

Key idea · Hacking

One of the main methods attackers use to gain access to computer networks, by specialist software and other tools. Intruders gain unauthorised access and take administrative control, so they can amend, copy and delete records, or even stop the network from operating.

Remember

Section 2Cyber Risk and Fraud›Topic 2.4
2.4

Distributed denial of service

A DDoS attack does not steal anything. It floods a website with so much traffic that real customers cannot get in.

Key idea · DDoS attack

Designed to disrupt an organisation's online presence by preventing legitimate users from accessing its online services, by overwhelming the website and communications links with a wave of internet traffic the system cannot handle.

Key term · Botnet

A large group of individual computers that criminals have previously compromised. On a signal from the organiser, they all send messages to the target at the same time.

A botnet waits for the signal

These computers were compromised earlier. Their owners do not know. Send the organiser's signal.

🕹️Attack organiser
🖥️shop-website.exampleworking

Server load is normal. Real customers can shop.

Remember
In simple words

Imagine thousands of people told to phone one small shop at the same moment. The line is always busy, so real customers cannot get through. That is a DDoS attack on a website.

Exam alert · Q3, Q7DDoS = an internet site shut down by a planned surge of traffic, organised through BOTNETS. It is not hacking or phishing.
Case answer · Q30DDoS disrupts online presence by overwhelming the website and communication links with traffic it cannot handle. Organised through botnets: on the organiser's signal, all compromised machines message the target at once.
Exam alert · Q15DDoS attacks are designed to disrupt an organisation's ONLINE PRESENCE by blocking legitimate users.

Preventing and tackling cyberattacks

Most attacks are stopped by simple habits done every time.

1 · Strong passwords, never reused

Tip from the book: password management apps can suggest and remember secure passwords.

2 · Security software: anti-virus and two-factor authentication

Install security software such as anti-virus (see the malware demo in 1.2) and two-factor authentication. Two-factor authentication verifies the user at log in by sending a one-time code to a second device, such as a smartphone.
