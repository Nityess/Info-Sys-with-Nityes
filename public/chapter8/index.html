Information systems

A factory turns materials and labour into products. A business turns data into information the same way, through its information systems. Explore one below.

Tap any part of the diagram

A student result system

Data comes in from the environment, is processed inside the boundary, and leaves as information.

IS is the entire system. IT is the technology used within it.

Key terms

A system
A set of interacting components that operate together to accomplish a purpose.
A business system
A collection of people, machines and methods organised to accomplish a set of specific functions.
Information systems (IS)
All systems and procedures involved in the collection, storage, production and distribution of information.
Information technology (IT)
The equipment used to capture, store, transmit or present information. IT provides a large part of the information systems infrastructure.
Information management
The process of planning, organizing, controlling, and using information and information systems effectively to support business objectives. It asks: what information does the business need, what technology should be used, how should information be stored and protected, and how should systems be improved?

Section 1Types of Management Information Systems›Topic 1.4.1
1.4.1

Expert systems

A computer system that stores specialised knowledge and decision-making rules, uses them to solve problems in one domain, and helps users decide like a human expert. It suggests; it does not replace people.

Loan application · set the facts
Knowledge base · rules (illustrative)

The two components

Knowledge base
Contains the expert's set of decision-making rules, facts and specialised knowledge about the domain.
Inference engine
Applies the rules from the knowledge base to the specific circumstances entered by the user to reach a conclusion or suggestion.

Where they are used

When and why

Best used when
The problem is reasonably well-defined, it cannot be solved by conventional transaction processing, and experts are often highly paid.
Why businesses use them (PQ1)
Relative cost (the main reason), wider accessibility, avoids the learning curve, saves time, consistency.

Section 2Control of Management Information Systems›Topic 2.7.1
2.7.1

IT controls: general IT and information processing

In a computerised environment, controls are manual and automated, and come in two layers. General IT controls protect the whole IT environment. Information processing controls protect the data inside one application. Tap either layer.

Rule: affects the whole IT environment = general IT. Relates to one application's transactions = information processing.
Two layers

Tap a top badge or an application stage

Badges along the top are general IT controls. The stages inside each application are information processing controls.

Key terms

Information processing controls
Controls relating to the processing of information in IT applications or manual processes that directly address risks to the integrity of information, i.e. the completeness, accuracy and validity of transactions. In simple words: data entered and processed is correct and reliable.
General IT controls (GITCs)
Controls over the entity's IT processes that support the continued proper operation of the IT environment, including the continued effective functioning of information processing controls and the integrity of information. They cover system stability, security and access, and support for IP controls. In simple words: the IT system itself keeps running properly and securely.

Section 3Risk Management and Control of IT›Topic 3.2.4
3.2.4

Integrity controls

Data keeps its integrity if it is complete and not corrupted. Input controls ensure the accuracy, completeness and validity of input. One typing mistake, two kinds of check.

Illustrative example

4,500 was typed wrongly

Verification compares the entry with the paper it came from. Validation lets the computer test whether the entry is sensible. Try both on each mistake.

Key terms

Data verification
Ensuring data entered matches source documents. A faithful copy of the original: completeness and accuracy of transcription.
Data validation
Ensuring data entered is not incomplete or unreasonable, using programmed checks. Data is plausible, in range and correctly formatted.
